
#### Routes of Administration

**Enteral Medications** enter the body through the digestive system.
**Parenteral Medications** enter the body by some other means.
**Absorption** is the process by which medications travel through body tissues to the bloodstream.

| Route             | Rate      |
| ----------------- | --------- |
| **Enteral**       |           |
| Sublingual(SL)    | Rapid     |
| Per rectum(PR)    | Rapid     |
| By mouth(PO)      | Slow      |
| **Parenteral**    |           |
| Intravenous(IV)   | Immediate |
| Intraosseous(IO)  | Immediate |
| Inhalation        | Rapid     |
| Intranasal(IN)    | Rapid     |
| Intramuscular(IM) | Moderate  |
| Subcutaneous(SC)  | Slow      |
| Transcutaneous    | Slow      |

#### Medication Forms
The form of medication usually dictates the route of administration. The manufacturer chooses the form to ensure:
-  Proper route administration
-  Timing of the medication's release into the bloodstream
-  Effects on the target organs or body systems

##### **Basic Forms**
-  Tablets and capsules
-  Solutions and suspensions
-  Metered-dose inhalers
-  Topical medications
-  Transcutaneous medications
-  Gels
-  Gases for inhalation

##### **Tablets and Capsules**
**Capsules** are gelatin shells filled with powdered or liquid medication. **Tablets** often contain other materials that are mixed with the medication and compressed. 

Must swallowed and digested.
-  Slower route
-  Less useful in emergency

##### Solutions and Suspensions
A **solution** is a liquid mixture of substances.
-  **Mixture** will not separate by filtering or letting it stand.
-  Many solutions can be given as an IV, IM, or SC injection.

A **suspension** is a substance that does not dissolve well in liquids.
-  Will separate if it stands or is filtered
-  Important to shake or swirl before administration
-  Usually administered by mouth
	-  antibiotic for pediatric patient or activated charcoal
-  Occasionally given rectally or applied directly to the skin
	-  calamine lotion
- May be given via IM or SC injection
	-  hormone shots or vaccinations

##### Metered-Dose Inhalers
Liquids or solids broken into small enough droplets or particles may be inhaled. A **Metered-Dose Inhaler(MDI)** directs such substances through mouth into lungs. Delivers the same amount each time.

##### Topical Medications
Includes lotions, creams, and ointments. Applied to skin surface and affects only that area. Example:
-  Lotion: calamine lotion
-  Cream: hydrocortisone cream
-  Ointment: Neoporin ointment
Lotions contain more water and are absorbed <span style="color: var(--color-yellow)">rapidly</span>
Ointments contain less water and are absorbed <span style="color: var(--color-red)">slowly</span>

##### Transcutaneous Medications
-  Designed to be absorbed through the skin. 
-  Also referred to as ***transdermal***
-  May have systemic effects
Examples:
-  Nitroglycerin past
-  Adhesive patch
<span style="color: var(--color-red)"><u>If you **touch** the medication with your skin, you will absorb it just like the patient</u>.</span>

##### Gels
-  Semi-liquid
-  Administered in capsules or through plastic tubes
Example: oral glucose for patients with diabetes

##### Gases for Inhalation
-  Outside the operating room, most commonly used is oxygen
-  Usually delivered through a nonrebreathing mask or nasal cannula
