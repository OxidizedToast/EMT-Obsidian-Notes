
#### General Steps in Administering Medications
-  Medications should be administered **only under authorization of medical control**
-  Follow the "six rights of medication administration"
-  Medication errors **almost** always result from failure to follow these "six rights"
##### The "Six Rights" of Medication Administration
1.  **Right Patient**
2.  **Right Medication**
3.  **Right Dose**
4.  **Right Route**
5.  **Right Time**
6.  **Right Documentation**

#### Medication Administration and the EMT
**Medications the Unit/You may carry:**
-  Oxygen
-  Oral glucose
-  Activated charcoal
-  Aspirin
-  Epinephrine
-  Narcan

##### Circumstances in which medication may be administered:
- Peer-assisted administration
	- Administer to yourself or partner
-  Patient-assisted administration
	- You assist patient with their own medications
- EMT-administered medications
##### Determined by:
-  State and local protocols
-  Medical control

###### Medication used by EMTs
The state, department, and medical director will define which medications are carried on your ambulance.

#### <span style="color: var(--color-orange)">Oral Medications</span>
##### <span style="color: var(--color-green)">Advantages</span>
-  Ease of access
-  Comfort level
##### <span style="color: var(--color-red)">Disadvantages</span>
-  The digestive tract can be easily affected by foods, stress, and illness.
-  Speed of movement of food through the tract dramatically changes the speed of absorption.

If using oral medication you must, <u>confirm that the patient has a patent airway</u> and is able to swallow, and then instruct the patient to swallow or chew the medication. If they can swallow and talk oral is usually safe, but <span style="color: var(--color-red)"><u>do not give to someone unresponsive</u></span> as they can choke.

##### <span style="color: var(--color-purple)">Activated Charcoal</span>
-  Reduces the amount of medication being absorbed; the charcoal absorbed bits of the medication
-  Ground into fine powder
-  Frequently suspended with sorbitol(a sugar alcohol with a sweet taste)
-  Administered by mouth(swallowed)
-  <span style="color: var(--color-red)">**Do not** give to patients with altered level of consciousness</span>

##### <span style="color: var(--color-purple)">Oral Glucose</span>
-  A simple sugar that cells use for energy
-  Treats diabetics that are hypoglycemic, raises blood sugar; make sure patient is able to keep blood sugar up
-  EMTs give it only by mouth
-  <span style="color: var(--color-red)">Do not give to an unconscious patient or one who cannot protect the airway.</span> The gel can run down the back of the throat and cause patient to choke.

##### <span style="color: var(--color-purple)">Aspirin</span>
-  Reduces fever, pain, and inflammation
-  Inhibits platelet aggregation
	-  Useful during heart attack
	-  Platelets clump together to form blood-clots, aspirin causes the platelets to slide off each other, won't remove clot but will prevent from getting larger
-  Contraindications
	-  <span style="color: var(--color-red)">Hypersensitivity to aspirin</span>
	- <span style="color: var(--color-red)"> Liver damage, bleeding disorder, asthma</span>
	-  <span style="color: var(--color-red)">Should not be given to children</span>


#### <span style="color: var(--color-orange)">Sublingual Medications</span>
##### <span style="color: var(--color-green)">Advantages</span>
-  Easy to advise patients
-  Quick absorption
##### <span style="color: var(--color-red)">Disadvantages</span>
-  Constant evaluation of the airway
-  Possible choking
-  Not for uncooperative or unconscious patients

##### <span style="color: var(--color-purple)">Nitroglycerin (Nitro)</span>
-  Relieves angina pain(cardiac patients)
-  Increases blood flow
-  Relaxes veins
Nitro dilates the blood vessels which increased blood flow
###### Before administering:
-  Check blood pressure
	-  Systolic less than 100 may be harmful; can make blood pressure drop more
-  Obtain order to administer
-  Possibility of Myocardial infraction(MI), if no relief
-  <span style="color: var(--color-red)">Should not be used with erectile dysfunction medications within in 24 hours(Viagra, Cialis, Levitra); they do similar things to Nitro</span>
-  <span style="color: var(--color-yellow)">May cause mild headache/burning under tongue</span>

###### Administration by tablet
-  Sublingually under the tongue
-  Slight tingling or burning
-  Storage is important; <span style="color: var(--color-yellow)">light sensitive</span>
###### Administration by metered-dose spray
-  On or under tongue
-  One spray = one tablet

###### Administration considerations (for both table and spray):
-  Wait 5 minutes before repeating dose
-  Monitor vital signs; especially blood pressure
-  Wear gloves
-  Know local protocols

#### <span style="color: var(--color-orange)">Intramuscular Medications</span>

##### <span style="color: var(--color-green)">Advantages</span>
-  Quick, easy access without using vein
-  Stable blood flow to muscles
##### <span style="color: var(--color-red)">Disadvantages</span>
-  Use of a needle (and subsequent pain)
-  Patients may fear pain or injury

##### <span style="color: var(--color-purple)">Epinephrine</span>
-  Controls fight-or-flight response
-  Primary medication for delivery IM
-  Also called adrenaline, released inside the body under stress
-  Sympathomimetic

-  Increases heart rate and constricts blood vessels
-  <span style="color: var(--color-red)">Do not give to patient with hypertension, hypothermia, MI, or not wheezing due to allergic reaction.</span>
-  May be delivered with an auto-injector to treat life-threatening anaphylaxis

##### What it does
-  Allergens causes body to over-release histamine
-  Epinephrine acts as an antidote to reverse the effects of histamine
-  Dispensed from an auto-injector

##### <span style="color: var(--color-purple)">Naloxone (Narcan)</span>
-  Antagonist
-  Used to reverse the effects of an opioid overdose
-  Given when not breathing well
###### Important considerations:
-  Consult medical control
-  Effects may not last as long as opioids; repeated doses may be necessary
-  Can cause severe withdrawal symptoms including seizures and cardiac arrest
-  Consider your safety

#### <span style="color: var(--color-orange)">Intranasal Medications</span>
##### <span style="color: var(--color-purple)">Naloxone</span>
-  The most common technique for naloxone administration is via the intranasal route
-  Administer half dose (1 mg) per nostril
-  Given when not breathing well

#### <span style="color: var(--color-orange)">Inhalation Medications</span>
##### <span style="color: var(--color-purple)">Oxygen</span>
-  All cells need it to survive
-  Generally administered via:
	-  Nonrebreathing mask
	-  Nasal cannula
	-  BVM
##### <span style="color: var(--color-purple)">MDIs and nebulizers</span>
-  Liquid turned into mist or spray
-  Medication breathed in and delivered to alveoli
-  Fast absorption rate
-  Easy route to access
-  Portable
###### <span style="color: var(--color-purple)">Spacer</span>
- Use a spacer to avoid spray misdirection
	-  Fits over the inhaler like a sleeve
	-  Patient sprays the prescribed dose into the chamber and then breathes on and out of the mouthpiece
	-  Especially useful with young children
###### <span style="color: var(--color-purple)">Small-volume nebulizers</span>
-  Easier to use than MDIs
	-  Take longer to deliver medication
	-  Require an external oxygen source
	-  More effective in patients with moderate to severe respiratory distress
	-  Can be used while a patients on CPAP and during BVM ventilation

###### <span style="color: var(--color-yellow)">Patients use:</span>
-  "Rescue inhaler" MDIs to relieve bronchial spasms quickly
-  Maintenance or controller inhaler
-  The only medication that is effective during an acute attack of shortness of breath is fast-acting rescue inhalers

#### <span style="color: var(--color-red)">Medication Errors</span>
-  Inappropriate use of a medication that could lead to patient harm
-  May be possible to minimize errors if circumstances are understood
-  Ensure the environment does not contribute to errors
##### If a medication error occurs:
-  Provide appropriate patient care.
-  Notify medical control.
-  Follow protocols.
-  Document thoroughly, accurately, and honestly.
-  Talk with your partner, supervisor, or medical director.
